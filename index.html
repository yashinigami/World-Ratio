AFTER THE BASICS

A few ways people approach investing

Strategies are frameworks, not shortcuts or guaranteed outcomes. Each comes with trade-offs.

01↗

Buy and hold

Investors buy an asset with a long-term view and hold through shorter market swings, usually because they believe its value can grow over time.

Keep in mind: Long holding periods do not remove the risk of loss. The business and your reasons for owning it still matter.

02◌

Rupee-cost averaging

A fixed amount is invested at regular intervals. When prices are lower, the same amount buys more units; when prices are higher, it buys fewer.

Keep in mind: It can support disciplined investing, but it does not guarantee a profit or protect against falling markets.

03✿

Diversification

Money is spread across different companies, sectors or asset types so the outcome does not depend on just one holding.

Keep in mind: Diversification can reduce concentration risk, but it cannot eliminate market risk.

04⌕

Value investing

Investors look for securities they believe are priced below a reasonable estimate of their underlying worth, based on research and assumptions.

Keep in mind: An apparently cheap stock may be cheap for a reason. Valuation is uncertain and requires careful analysis.

Learning material only — not personal investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell any security.

WR.

The financial history of the world, made explorable.

"World Ratio (Finance)" is an independent financial-history exploration project developed by an MSc Finance student based in India.

It was created to make learning about financial history easier, more accessible and more engaging by connecting financial events with the places, institutions and people they have affected.

From major financial crises and government policies to local economic developments, World Ratio aims to help people understand how financial decisions and events have shaped the world.

RESEARCH PRINCIPLES

Evidence before volume.

Events are stored as structured records with source links. The initial dataset is intentionally small. An empty location or year means the current sample contains no verified record — it does not mean nothing happened there.

  • Prefer official reports, regulators, central banks and primary records.
  • Use reputable journalism and academic research for context.
  • Distinguish facts, estimates, interpretation and causal claims.
  • Never invent a financial event to fill a map.
